Chip, wafer, and manufacturing

The opening is a conceptual multi-die AI accelerator package, informed by visible structures in NVIDIA Blackwell and AMD MI300 packages. It is not an exact product replica or a claim that a particular AI accelerator was made in Austin. Component sizes, layers, separation distances, and silicon surface patterns are illustrative. The die-to-wafer transition traces a die’s origin backward through manufacturing. Gas particles and additional wafers explain processes and production growth qualitatively; they are not measured emissions or a quantitative production chart.

The flight from orbit to the neighborhood

The flight follows one camera from orbit to residential streets and back out to Travis County. The global view comes from NASA Blue Marble. Regional and local views combine NASA GIBS imagery with historical aerial photographs from USDA and USGS. Overlapping images are aligned by coordinates and color-matched for the flight. Map services and data available from U.S. Geological Survey, National Geospatial Program.

The camera passes downtown Austin, the Samsung Austin campus on Samsung Boulevard, and residential streets to its west. The facility position comes from the air-permit application. This view establishes the setting. The health research that follows covers four anonymized facilities across Travis County.

Jobs and the training bench

Impact DataSource (2025), p. 6, reports 4,907 direct jobs at Samsung’s Austin campus in 2024 and estimates a further 11,291 indirect and induced jobs. The lengths on the common scale sum to 16,198 jobs supported. The report’s model uses company information and regional multipliers.

The soldering station and its circuit board are original three-dimensional illustrations based on Skillpoint Alliance’s description of its training equipment.
